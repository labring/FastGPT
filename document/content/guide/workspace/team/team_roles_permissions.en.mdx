## Team Management Tabs

Open **Account → Team management** from the left navigation bar. The page is organized into four tabs—**Members**, **Departments**, **Groups**, and **Permissions**:

- **Members**: View all active team members, statuses, and the Team Owner badge; click **Invite Members** in the upper-right corner to generate invitation links, remove members, or transfer team ownership.
- **Departments**: Build a hierarchical organization tree and assign members to departments for inherited access control.
- **Groups**: Create cross-departmental member groups (including the built-in default group named after the team) for batch authorization.
- **Permissions**: Grant team-level creation and administration permissions across members, departments, and groups.

## Permission System Overview

FastGPT's permission system combines **attribute-based** and **role-based** access control, providing fine-grained permission management for team collaboration. Through **members, departments, and groups**, you can flexibly configure access to teams, apps, and datasets.

## Teams

Membership follows the deployment's [team mode](/en/guide/admin/teamMode). In multi-team mode, users can join several teams and receive an initial team when created. In single-team mode, new users join the root user's team. Sync mode follows the external member system. The current interface provides an entrance for joining an existing team.

## Permission Management

FastGPT offers three permission management levels:

**Member Permissions**: Highest priority, directly assigned to individuals

**Department & Group Permissions**: Use union logic, lower priority than member permissions

Permission evaluation follows this logic:

1. Use an individual permission record when present, including an explicit value of `0`.
2. When the individual record is absent, combine matching permissions from member groups, the default all-members group, the member's departments, and ancestor departments using bitwise OR.
3. When all sources lack matching records, use the resource default supplied by the caller.

### Collaborators

Before assigning permissions to members, departments, or groups, add them as **collaborators** on the target resource:

- **Team-level permissions**: Navigate to **Account → Team management → Permissions** in the left navigation bar, click **Add**, select the target members, departments, or groups, and check the corresponding team permission boxes.
- **App and Dataset permissions**: Open the target App or Dataset, open the **Collaborators** dialog, and assign Read, Write, or Administrator permissions to the selected members, departments, or groups.

## Special Permissions

The table below clarifies how the three privileged roles (Admin, Owner, and Root) are assigned and how to identify your current role in the interface:

| Role | Definition and Assignment | How to Verify in the UI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Root (System Super Admin)** | Built-in system administrator account in self-hosted deployments with username `root` (initial password configured via `DEFAULT_ROOT_PSW`). FastGPT Cloud is operated by the official platform team and does not expose a tenant `root` account. | Check whether the **Admin** (Platform Administration Console) entry appears at the bottom of the left navigation bar, or verify whether the username under **Account → Profile** is `root`. |
| **Owner** | **Team Owner**: The account that created the team (`root` by default in single-team mode, or the registering user in cloud and multi-team modes).<br />**Resource Owner**: The member who created the specific App, Dataset, or Collection. | **Team Owner**: Open **Account → Team management → Members** and locate the member labeled **Owner**.<br />**Resource Owner**: Open the resource's **Collaborators** dialog and check for the **Owner** role badge. |
| **Admin (Administrator)** | **Team Admin**: A member, department, or group granted the **Admin** permission by the Team Owner.<br />**Resource Admin**: A collaborator granted **Administrator** access on a specific App, Dataset, or Collection by its Owner. | **Team Admin**: Open **Account → Team management → Permissions** and check whether the **Admin** column is enabled for your account.<br />**Resource Admin**: Open the resource's **Collaborators** dialog and check whether your permission level is **Administrator**. |

### Admin Permissions

Team Admins manage member invitations, removals, departments, groups, and team permissions; Resource Admins manage collaborator access on individual Apps or Datasets. Admin permissions observe the following boundaries:

- Cannot modify or remove their own Admin permissions.
- Cannot modify or remove the permissions of other peer Admins.
- Cannot grant Admin permissions to other collaborators (only the Owner or Root can appoint Admins).

### Owner Permissions

Every team and every resource (App, Dataset, or Collection) has a single unique Owner with full control and admin-appointment rights. An Owner can transfer ownership to another team member, relinquishing exclusive ownership upon transfer. In folder hierarchies, a parent folder's Owner inherits `Can Manage` (`manage`) permission on child resources while each child resource keeps its own single Owner; when a member is granted access only to a descendant resource, ancestor folders appear as read-only navigation entries.

### Root Permissions

`root` is the sole system-level super administrator account in self-hosted deployments, responsible for global model channels, system plugins, tenant quotas, and platform configuration, with full access and management rights within its team.

## Tips

### 1. Set Default Team Permissions

Use the default group (shown with the team name in the UI) to quickly set baseline permissions for the entire team. For example, grant everyone access to an app.

**Note**: Individual member permissions override default group permissions. For example, if App A grants edit permission to the default group, but User M is individually set to use-only, User M receives use-only access.

### 2. Batch Permission Management

Create groups or organizations to efficiently manage permissions for multiple users. Add users to a group, then grant permissions to the entire group.
